# Methodology notes from the pilot batch (Stonehenge + Aleppo)

Written while researching, as requested — things that looked fine on paper but caused real friction or genuine ambiguity against real sites. None of these were silently worked around; each is either flagged inline in the dossiers or listed here with the workaround I used.

## 1. Serial/multi-location UNESCO inscriptions don't fit a one-site-one-point model
"Stonehenge" is colloquially one site, but its actual UNESCO inscription — "Stonehenge, Avebury and Associated Sites" — is a *serial* listing bundling multiple separate locations (Stonehenge, Avebury, and other monuments up to ~17 miles apart) under one UNESCO ID. The dossier schema's `coordinates` field assumes a single lat/lng point per site. I profiled Stonehenge alone (matching what you asked for and what most visitors search for) and noted the serial relationship in `alternate_names`, but this is a real, recurring case — UNESCO has dozens of serial/transnational listings — and the data model should have an explicit answer: one dossier per physical monument (my approach here) with a shared `unesco_id`, or one dossier per UNESCO inscription with multiple coordinate points. Worth deciding deliberately rather than by pilot-site precedent.

## 2. Chronic, undated threats don't fit the `first_identified_date` field
Several real threats (Stonehenge's traffic/setting pressure, Aleppo's pre-war overpopulation/development pressure) are genuinely chronic with no clean "this is when it started" date in any source. Forcing a date would have meant inventing one. I added an ad hoc `date_note` field to both dossiers as a workaround — this isn't in the approved schema and shouldn't be treated as adopted; it's a proposal. Recommend the schema formally allow `first_identified_date: null` with an optional free-text note, rather than requiring a specific date.

## 3. Tier classification is ambiguous for quasi-governmental heritage bodies
English Heritage (Stonehenge's day-to-day manager) is a charity that operates historic sites on behalf of the UK government — not quite a "national heritage ministry" (Tier 1) and not quite an "NGO" in the usual sense (Tier 2 as written). I classified it as Tier 2 for this pilot, but the source hierarchy definitions don't clearly cover this category, and it will recur (many countries have similar arm's-length heritage trusts). Worth an explicit rule.

## 4. The clearest real problem: the status decision tree doesn't say what "recent" means for old, unrepaired damage
This is the significant one. Critical Concern's first trigger ("confirmed destruction, severe damage... per an authoritative source") has no recency qualifier. High Concern's second trigger explicitly requires a damaging event "within roughly the last 5 years." Aleppo's core damage happened 2012–2016 — outside that 5-year window today — but a large share of it (per 2026 reporting, ~60% of the souks) is still unrepaired. Two defensible readings exist:
- **(a)** Critical Concern's undated trigger still applies as long as the damage remains unrepaired, regardless of how long ago it happened — a site could stay Critical for decades absent a formal "repaired" determination.
- **(b)** Status should track *present, current* risk more than historical scars — once a site is stabilized, being actively and successfully restored, and not under acute active threat, it should be able to move down from Critical even with visible unrepaired damage remaining.

I proposed **High Concern** for Aleppo under reading (b), treating it similarly to how the methodology document's own Angkor case study frames genuine improvement over time — but I want to flag clearly that this was my judgment call under real ambiguity, not the tree producing a single obvious answer. This is worth resolving explicitly (e.g., "Critical Concern's damage trigger applies only while the underlying event is within the same recency window as High Concern's 5-year rule, after which the classification is re-evaluated on present-day risk factors alone") before this comes up on a future site where the call matters more.

## 5. Source tier doesn't capture within-tier reliability
One claim (the Umayyad Mosque's partial reopening) came from a small Syria travel-guide website — clearly not Tier 1/2, and journalism-adjacent enough that I tier-3'd it, but I have real reservations about its editorial rigor that the tier system doesn't capture. I flagged it explicitly in the dossier (`reliability_note` field, also ad hoc) rather than silently including or excluding it. A future version of the validation script could support a lightweight reliability flag independent of tier, for exactly this situation.

## 6. Undated historical events had to be dropped, not approximated
Stonehenge has a much longer, well-documented tunnel-project history going back to 1995 (multiple proposed-then-cancelled schemes), but the only readily available secondary source covering the full timeline was Wikipedia — and the validation rule "no event relies solely on a Tier 4 source" correctly blocked me from including those older events without independent corroboration. I omitted them rather than approximate or use Wikipedia alone. This will happen often for older history on real sites; it's the validation rule working as intended, but it means early dossiers may have thinner historical timelines than the site's real history would support, until someone does the extra work of finding Tier 1–3 corroboration for older events.

None of the above required pausing the batch — I resolved each with an explicit, flagged judgment call or omission rather than a silent guess, per your instruction.
